## 2. BVI company records: available products and access limits

The ordinary company search is **US$50**. It provides an index of corporate filings, with copies of documents ordered separately. A later statutory schedule provides **US$250 inspection of all qualifying documents** held for one company. Neither service overrides restrictions on private registers. [R1–R3]

The target is **Gamifi Limited, BVI number 2082070**. The supplied screenshot establishes the returned name and number. It does not disclose status, incorporation date, agent, directors or bank information. The $50 price is corroborated by official sources; the screenshot itself does not show a price or currency. Statutory amounts are US dollars under the Act’s section 2 definition. [R4]

| Record / action | Access and government fee | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Basic entity lookup | Free identification stage shown by the supplied screenshot | Matching name/number; no good-standing determination. |

### What the history does—and does not—contain

FSC guidance revised **1 September 2025** defines the report’s history as transactions **filed with the Registry**. These are corporate filings. It is not a bank ledger, token-transfer history, revenue statement or custody record. Specific filed documents can be ordered using that index. The US$50 product therefore cannot alone identify the people behind the November transfers. [R1; final sentence is investigative inference]

The US$250 category is in **S.I.57/2024, section 2(d), pages 3–4**. It commenced **2 January 2025**, through the 2024 Amendment Act’s commencement notice. The still-linked **R820 is dated March 2023** and omits this newer category. Later S.I.70/2024, 62/2025 and 119/2025 were reviewed: they concern beneficial ownership and transitional filings, rather than replacing the ordinary inspection fees. [R2–R3, R12–R15]

### Public director names are a different product from historical control records

FSC’s legislative circular describes the public list as **current directors**. Its October 2023 newsletter gives the **R811** certification function and US$75 fee. The 2024 amendment expressly separates the full register, available to the company, its agent and specified authorities, from the list other people may request. [R6–R8]

The investigative implication is that today’s list cannot settle Laura’s authority in 2022. Dated director entries, appointment/resignation instruments, corporate instructions and key-custody records for that period remain necessary. A current officer name is not proof of who signed a historical transaction.

### Shareholder and financial records require a different route

Section **43A(5)–(6)** restricts the members register unless the company elects public availability. Company/agent, competent-authority and lawful law-enforcement access are expressly identified. Section **43A(9)** concerns members as of filing and subsequent changes; the new regime does not guarantee a publicly backfilled 2021–2022 ownership history. [R8]

Section **98A** sends annual financial returns to the **registered agent**, who must supply a copy to the FSC or another competent authority on request. The prescribed form contains balance-sheet and income-statement summaries. The regime began **1 January 2023**, with the earliest returns becoming due in 2024; it is not a promised 2021–2022 transaction ledger. Later initial-filing extensions mean generic deadlines cannot establish GamiFi’s compliance status. [R9–R11, R16]

### Lawful routes that require no purchase by a researcher

- **Existing primary disclosures:** the registry screenshot, archived terms and litepaper remain evidence of what was publicly returned or stated. They cannot substitute for absent certificates.
- **Independently published records:** company/counterparty disclosures, court exhibits and liquidator notices may legitimately reproduce records otherwise obtained through the Registry. No extra GamiFi filing was recovered in this registry-access lane.
- **Existing entitled holders:** a company or agent may provide its own records. Section 100 grants directors inspection rights and members specified rights subject to statutory and constitutional limits. GMI token ownership alone does not establish shareholder status. [R4]
- **Investigators:** section 43A(5), section 118B(4) and section 98A(3) identify routes to restricted registers and annual returns. These are records to seek through appropriate powers, not free public-search products. [R8–R9]



## 3. Beneficial ownership: a separate, restricted route

The FSC announced that its legitimate-interest request functionality would launch on **1 April 2026**. This is a material change from early-2025 guidance that described access mainly for designated authorities. It does not turn beneficial ownership into a free, unrestricted name search. The public-search landing page now separately links to Beneficial Ownership Information. The automated reader could not open that linked login destination; no application or company-specific ownership result was obtained. [BO-01, BO-02]

The regulations require a demonstrable purpose connected to investigating, preventing or detecting money laundering, terrorist financing or proliferation financing, or another specified qualifying ground. They do not establish entitlement merely because someone is curious, lost money or holds tokens. An individual applicant must supply identification, contact information, the entity name, a signed purpose statement and a declaration limiting use to that purpose. The Registrar assesses eligibility and can refuse; disclosure is limited and subject to objections/exemptions. The disclosure provision covers natural persons meeting a 25% ownership/voting threshold or specified control tests, so it is not an all-shareholder list. [BO-03, regulations 26 and 31A–31G, PDF pp. 9–21]

For one entity, SI 119/2025 sets the legitimate-interest inspection/copy request fee at **US$75**. This is separate from the US$50 company search. The order's commencement is tied to SI 120/2025, which is deemed effective from 29 December 2025. There is no verified free substitute or fee waiver for this requester in the sources reviewed. The company, its registered agent, or an insolvent liquidator also has a distinct written-request route under regulation 31B(2A); that does not confer access on an unrelated investigator. [BO-04, pp. 2–3; BO-05, pp. 2, 4]

The March 2026 circular describes agent notification and objection stages and a 12-day operational processing timeline when there is no objection or opposition. This is not a guaranteed deadline for a disputed application. It also requires English PDF supporting documents and certified valid KYC documents. A request is therefore not an anonymous inquiry: it can alert the entity's registered agent. No request has been made. [BO-01]

**Application to GamiFi:** no evidence retrieved here establishes that Gamifi Limited has filed a BO entry, who appears in it, or whether this requester qualifies. If an eligible application ever proceeds, the target should be **Gamifi Limited, BVI company 2082070**, with a factual evidence summary and no unsupported claim that Laura personally received funds. Corporate ownership or management information could identify a records custodian or a person to ask about company decisions. It would still require independent, dated evidence to connect that person to a 2022 signing key, an exchange account or a particular payout. This is an investigative inference about the records' potential value, not a finding of attribution.


## 4. Gazette access: published policy and working free routes

**Historical liquidation notices are not uniformly inaccessible without payment.** The Gazette describes a paid archival subscription, but this review successfully downloaded older liquidation PDFs from publicly indexed URLs on its own host. The government also intentionally republishes selected complete Gazettes on its main website during service outages. Those are genuine free routes for particular issues; neither route provides a complete searchable corporate history.

### Published access rules versus observed access

| Material or function | Published access rule | What was verified in this review |
|---|---|---|
| Complete Gazette, first two months after publication | Free basic registered account | Official Help and FAQ describe this entitlement; no account was created. |
| Government and Statutory sections older than two months | Free basic account | Older Part 1 PDFs downloaded without credentials from public search results. |
| Separately listed attachments, including legislation | Remain free according to FAQ | Entitlement verified from FAQ; attachments are distinct from commercial notices. |
| Liquidation and Other sections older than two months | Enhanced subscription, advertised at $500 per year | Selected older Part 2 files nevertheless downloaded from their publicly indexed official URLs, without credentials. |
| Gazette website's own search and recent-publication menu | Website login required for search | Anonymous Recent Publications request returned HTTP 403; this is an access result, not evidence of a company notice. |
| Complete issues republished in government bulletins | Bulletin invites public review | Full 26 March and 30 July 2026 issues downloaded from gov.vg without registration. |

Sources: [GZ-01], [GZ-02], [GZ-04], [GZ-05], and downloaded PDFs [GZ-P01]–[GZ-P11]. The $500 is the amount displayed by the official Help page; it was not a checkout quote. No fee was paid. Basic registration asks for name, email and password. A free account is a useful next step, but it does not confer the published paid archival entitlement. [GZ-01]

### A particularly clear free example: March 2026 liquidation notices

The Cabinet Office's bulletin of 26 March 2026 explicitly attached Gazette No. 29 while the Gazette website was experiencing technical difficulties. The attached 72-page PDF remains downloadable in this review on 6 September 2026—more than two months after publication. Its cover identifies Liquidator Appointments at printed page 1013, Dissolution Notices at 1038, other liquidation notices at 1056 and company continuations at 1058. The commercial section starts at PDF page 26, printed page 1013. This directly disproves an absolute claim that every older liquidation notice requires buying a subscription. [GZ-04, GZ-P10]

A second Cabinet Office bulletin dated 31 July 2026 supplies the complete 78-page Gazette No. 80 of 30 July 2026. This is a currently recent issue, independently available through the government bulletin. Neither sample contains a text match for Gamifi or 2082070. [GZ-05, GZ-P11]

### Corporate-status information can also appear in the free Part 1

The official 16 June 2022 Part 1 contains Registrar notices warning that named companies may be struck off for failure to appoint a registered agent. Those notices begin at PDF page 23; the 15 December 2022 Part 1 contains similar warnings at PDF page 4. These are examples involving other companies, not GamiFi findings. They show why an investigation should examine Government and Statutory notices as well as the commercial liquidation section. A warning, completed strike-off, voluntary liquidation and dissolution are separate events and should be recorded separately if a matching notice is found. [GZ-P03, GZ-P06]
